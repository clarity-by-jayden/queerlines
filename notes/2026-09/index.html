Book: Oye ✦ Author guest: Melissa Mogollon

Here’s a recap of what we discussed at the latest Queer Between the Lines meetup.

TL;DR

We talked about Oye’s one-sided phone call format (and how it shapes what we know), the characters’ arcs (especially Luciana’s shift from anxious control → calm acceptance), and Abu as the emotional centre of the story. We were also joined by the author, Melissa Mogollon, who shared why she chose the format and how the italic sections work.

Highlights video

What we discussed (more detail)

1) The format: a one-sided phone call

  • A few of us found it disorienting at first (working out “who’s speaking”), but once it clicked, it felt immersive — like being dropped into a live conversation.
  • We talked about how the limitation actually becomes the point: we only ever get one person’s perspective, and we still build a strong sense of Mari as a presence (even without hearing her voice directly).

2) The italic sections (flashback / inner world)

Melissa shared that the biggest craft challenge was: how do readers learn what they need, if Luciana wouldn’t naturally say it out loud to Mari?

  • The italic sections became a way to show the “full scene in Luciana’s head” — memories, context, and internal thoughts — while the spoken parts are what she chooses to share.
  • We also noted how this creates an interesting layer: you can see what Luciana reveals vs. what she withholds.

3) Character arcs & relationships

  • Luciana: the arc we kept coming back to was anxious control → calmer acceptance, particularly toward the end when she starts respecting Abu’s wishes rather than trying to “save” her.
  • Abu: we spoke about how Abu feels like the true protagonist in many ways — starting as a funny caricature and then becoming more layered as her history is revealed.
  • Mari: we talked about how Mari can read as distant, but also as someone needing space to cope — and how that sibling dynamic can be brutally honest, then instantly loving.

4) The author Q&A (Melissa Mogollon)

Melissa talked about:

  • Why this unusual format was the only one that felt “right” for the story (she tried more traditional POVs, but they didn’t work).
  • How restrictions can be creatively energising (even if she wouldn’t necessarily write the next novel in the same structure).
  • Audiobook adaptation: the narrator uses a different tone for the italic sections, which some readers find clarifies the reading experience.
